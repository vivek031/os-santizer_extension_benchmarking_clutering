Gaps in the paper

  • No false-positive rate. The authors say judging a report is “deeply non-trivial” (§8.2).
  • The verdict depends on context. The same code is safe or exploitable depending on who runs it and who owns the files.
  • Duplicates are grouped by hand. 679K log entries in 5 days, about 244 copies of each finding.
  • Severity is fixed per pass. It depends on the access type (read, write, execute), not on how exploitable the situation is; related reports from different passes are not linked.
  • Reports are checked by hand. A person reads and filters them with grep, awk and sort: about 2 hours a week.

09 / 18

(~30 s) So far, the paper. Now the question we asked ourselves: if you really ran this tool, what would you do with its output? The authors are honest about it: they never measured false positives, because a report is only a real bug in the right context, like the Go example. And the raw output is noisy: hundreds of thousands of log lines, the same finding repeated more than 200 times, a severity that only looks at read, write or execute, and a person sorting it all by hand with grep and awk. That is the gap we picked up.
